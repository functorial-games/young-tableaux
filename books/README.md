# Books and substantial texts

This directory is a reference shelf, not a statement about the eventual interaction design.

Prefer authoritative publisher/author/university links. Do **not** commit book scans unless redistribution is clearly permitted.

## Core shelf

1. **William Fulton — _Young Tableaux: With Applications to Representation Theory and Geometry_**
   - Cambridge: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/young-tableaux/A7570B10D82AE7233E25E5D6F70A07B6
   - Particularly relevant: bumping/sliding, plactic monoid, RSK, Littlewood–Richardson, symmetric polynomials, symmetric-group and GL representation theory, flag varieties, Schubert varieties.
   - Related reading repository: https://github.com/walnut-burgundy/fulton

2. **Bruce E. Sagan — _The Symmetric Group: Representations, Combinatorial Algorithms, and Symmetric Functions_, 2nd ed.**
   - Springer: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-6804-6
   - Central reference for the characteristic-zero representation theory/combinatorics interface, tableaux algorithms, hook formula, symmetric functions, differential posets and growth ideas.

3. **Richard P. Stanley — _Enumerative Combinatorics, Volume 2_, 2nd ed.**
   - Cambridge: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/enumerative-combinatorics/360F1EEA6B91AE359EE489AC4145EF49
   - Large symmetric-functions chapter; RSK; Schur functions; jeu de taquin; Littlewood–Richardson material; extensive exercises and historical notes.

4. **I. G. Macdonald — _Symmetric Functions and Hall Polynomials_, 2nd ed.**
   - WorldCat bibliographic record: https://search.worldcat.org/title/30733523
   - Broad/deep symmetric-function reference: Schur functions, Hall–Littlewood functions, Hall polynomials, symmetric-group characters and much more.

5. **G. D. James and A. Kerber — _The Representation Theory of the Symmetric Group_**
   - Cambridge: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group/F0A56D6A57B500A0884BF599234E1BF2
   - Ordinary and modular representation theory; Young subgroups; irreducibles; characters.

6. **G. D. James — _The Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups_**
   - Springer LNM 682: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0067708
   - Compact classical treatment: diagrams, tableaux, tabloids, Specht modules, Young's rule and modular theory.

7. **Tullio Ceccherini-Silberstein, Fabio Scarabotti, Filippo Tolli — _Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups_**
   - Cambridge: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-groups/5210396B7CF3C4C958DEE321EB659215
   - Modern self-contained treatment including Littlewood–Richardson, Schur–Weyl, character formulas, partition algebras and the Vershik–Okounkov approach.

8. **William Fulton and Joe Harris — _Representation Theory: A First Course_**
   - Springer: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-0979-9
   - Broader representation-theory context for symmetric groups, GL_n, Schur functors and geometry.

9. **Pavel Etingof, Oleg Golberg, Sebastian Hensel, Tiankai Liu, Alex Schwendner, Dmitry Vaintrob, Elena Yudovina — _Introduction to Representation Theory_**
   - AMS: https://bookstore.ams.org/stml-59
   - Author page / legal manuscript link: https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/books.html
   - Broad representation-theory reference useful when a tableaux construction needs its ambient algebra explained.

10. **Alain Lascoux — _Symmetric Functions and Combinatorial Operators on Polynomials_**
    - AMS CBMS 99: https://bookstore.ams.org/CBMS/99
    - Symmetric functions, divided differences, Schubert-polynomial/combinatorial operator viewpoint.

## Nearby combinatorics

11. **Anders Björner and Francesco Brenti — _Combinatorics of Coxeter Groups_**
    - Springer: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-27596-7
    - Type A is the symmetric group; useful for weak/Bruhat order, Coxeter generators, reduced words and the wider family around S_n.

12. **Drew Armstrong — _Generalized Noncrossing Partitions and Combinatorics of Coxeter Groups_**
    - AMS: https://pubs.ams.org/ebooks/memo/0949/
    - arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611106
    - Type-A specialization makes the symmetric-group connection explicit; useful as a bridge toward noncrossing/Coxeter toys.

13. **Richard P. Stanley — _Enumerative Combinatorics, Volume 1_, 2nd ed.**
    - Cambridge: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/enumerative-combinatorics/3155CDE1D973D49F873BDE2EAF8D7651
    - Posets, P-partitions, permutation statistics, promotion/evacuation and background that feeds directly into tableaux.

## Geometry / Schubert side

14. **William Fulton — _Young Tableaux_** remains the cleanest single bridge in this shelf from combinatorics to Grassmannians, flags and Schubert calculus.
15. For a dedicated geometric reading trail, cross-link the Fulton material in https://github.com/walnut-burgundy/fulton rather than duplicating that repository's source records here.

## Self-similarity / recursion

16. **Tom Leinster — general theory of self-similarity series**
    - Overview: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411343
    - Part I: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411344
    - Part II (recognition): https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411345
    - Merged/superseding paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4474
    - [Detailed reading notes](leinster-self-similarity.md)
    - Formalizes recursive gluing as systems of equations in spaces, constructs universal solutions as terminal coalgebras, and gives recognition theorems based on shrinking recursive pieces. Useful background for recursive/subdivision interactions even though the theory itself is topological rather than metric.

## How to extend this shelf

Good candidates for later additions:
- Schur–Weyl duality and polynomial representations of GL_n;
- Schubert calculus / Schubert polynomials;
- modular representations of S_n;
- Hecke algebras and q-analogues;
- crystals and tableau models;
- shifted, oscillating, ribbon and k-tableaux;
- random partitions / Plancherel measure;
- affine symmetric groups and affine Grassmannian combinatorics.

When adding a book, record:
- exact title and authors;
- edition/year if relevant;
- authoritative link;
- legal open version if the author/publisher supplies one;
- 1–3 lines saying why it belongs here.
